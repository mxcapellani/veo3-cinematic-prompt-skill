# Cinematography

Start with what the viewer needs to understand or feel. A wide view locates action, a medium view relates gesture and space, a close view emphasizes performance. Do not add all scales to a single instant.

A documentary treatment may use restrained handheld motion and practical light; advertising may use controlled surfaces and a deliberate reveal. Suspense can come from withheld information in framing and sound, without incoherent camera motion. Stylization is valid when requested and must not be overridden by realism defaults.

Describe concrete visual causes: a reflection reveals a vehicle edge, a doorway frames an arrival, a gaze directs attention to a prop. Avoid technical names that add no observable direction.
